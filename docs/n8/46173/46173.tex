The reported improvement retains all sixteen closed trails of Jakub Halfar's
selected instance of Jay Pantone's construction: four large trails arising
from four walks through 27 source loops each, and twelve trails completing the
remaining source loops. No trail is split or deleted, and no \emph{designated}
permutation window within a trail is discarded. Only the order of the trails
and their cyclic opening positions change. The selection of source loops and
the search program are Halfar's; the experiment reported here is a boundary
reassembly, not a new all-order construction.

\section*{Definitions and conventions}
A \emph{superpermutation of order \(n\)} is a finite word containing all \(n!\)
permutations of its alphabet as contiguous substrings. An \emph{\(n\)-window}
is any substring of length \(n\). It is \emph{valid} if its \(n\) symbols are
distinct and hence form a permutation, and \emph{dirty} otherwise. A
\emph{repeated permutation occurrence} is a valid window beyond the first
occurrence of its permutation.

We work with an alphabet of \(n\) distinct symbols and put \(h=n-3\).
For the computation, \(n=8\), \(h=5\), and the alphabet is
\(\{0,1,2,3,4,5,6,7\}\). A \emph{cyclic spelling} is a word read around a
circle; its positions are understood modulo its length. The \emph{source
loops} and \emph{closed trails} refer to the cyclic components and their
assembled walks in the selected construction. A \emph{trail opening} cuts a
cyclic spelling and extends it just far enough to preserve every designated
permutation window on that trail.

\section*{1. Phase determines two literal boundaries}
For general \(n\geq4\), let trail \(i\) have cyclic spelling length \(R_i\).
An admissible opening \(\alpha\) selects a cyclic starting position for the
first designated \(n\)-window. Let \(g_{i,\alpha}\) be the forward distance
(in letters) from the last designated window's start to the first one's
start, measured across the cut. In coordinates starting at the first
window, the last starts at position \(R_i-g_{i,\alpha}\). Thus the linear
piece containing both complete windows has length
\[
\ell_{i,\alpha}
=(R_i-g_{i,\alpha})+n
=R_i+h+a_i(\alpha),
\qquad a_i(\alpha):=3-g_{i,\alpha}.
\]
The number \(a_i(\alpha)\) is the \emph{opening adjustment} relative to the
baseline \(R_i+h\). For the openings in this experiment, a
\emph{weight-\(w\) opening} means one with gap \(g=w\). Consequently,
weight-3 openings have \(a=0\), and weight-2 openings have \(a=1\).

Write \(P_{i,\alpha}\) for the resulting opened piece. Its starting and
ending \(h\)-letter words are
\[
S_{i,\alpha}=\operatorname{prefix}_h(P_{i,\alpha}),
\qquad
E_{i,\alpha}=\operatorname{suffix}_h(P_{i,\alpha}).
\]
Here \emph{phase} denotes the complete cyclic start position, not merely
its residue modulo \(h\) (the \emph{coarse clock phase}). Distinct openings
with the same coarse phase can expose different \(S\) and \(E\).

\section*{2. One boundary-cost matrix; coupled opening states}
Let \(\mathcal B_n\) be the set of ordered \(h\)-letter words with distinct
symbols from the alphabet. Since \(h=n-3\),
\[
|\mathcal B_n|=n(n-1)\cdots(n-h+1)=\frac{n!}{3!},
\qquad |\mathcal B_8|=6\,720.
\]
For \(E,S\in\mathcal B_n\), define the longest suffix-to-prefix overlap
and the resulting boundary cost:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{ov}(E,S)
 &=\max\bigl\{k\in\{0,\ldots,h\}:
 \operatorname{suffix}_k(E)=\operatorname{prefix}_k(S)\bigr\},\\
D[E,S]&=h-\operatorname{ov}(E,S).
\end{aligned}
\]
The length-zero prefix and suffix are both the empty word. The matrix
\(D\in\mathbb R^{|\mathcal B_n|\times|\mathcal B_n|}\) is \emph{directed}:
in general, \(D[E,S]\neq D[S,E]\). It can be evaluated entry by entry
without allocating a dense array.

For a boundary word \(b\), let \(\mathbf e_b\) be its \emph{one-hot vector},
with one entry equal to 1 at the index of \(b\) in \(\mathcal B_n\) and all
other entries equal to 0. Thus an actual join from \(E\) to \(S\) costs
\[
\mathbf e_E^T D\,\mathbf e_S=D[E,S].
\]
Let \(\mathcal T=\{0,1,\ldots,15\}\) be the set of trail IDs,
matching the zero-based saved plan. Suppose trail \(i\in\mathcal T\)
has \(m_i\) admissible openings, labeled
\(\alpha=1,\ldots,m_i\). Define the matrices whose columns are its
possible boundary vectors, and its vector of opening adjustments:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\mathsf E_i&=
 [\,\mathbf e_{E_{i,1}}\ \cdots\ \mathbf e_{E_{i,m_i}}\,],\\
\mathsf S_i&=
 [\,\mathbf e_{S_{i,1}}\ \cdots\ \mathbf e_{S_{i,m_i}}\,],\\
\mathbf a_i&=(a_i(1),\ldots,a_i(m_i))^T.
\end{aligned}
\]
The complete table of interactions between opening choices of distinct
trails \(i,j\) is
\[
\boxed{C_{ij}=\mathsf E_i^T D\,\mathsf S_j},
\qquad
C_{ij}[\alpha,\beta]
=D[E_{i,\alpha},S_{j,\beta}].
\]

An \emph{opening-choice vector} \(x_i\in\{0,1\}^{m_i}\) has exactly one
nonzero entry: \(\mathbf1^T x_i=1\). The nonzero entry selects a single
opening of trail \(i\), fixing \emph{both} ends at once. Let
\(\sigma:\{1,\ldots,16\}\to\mathcal T\) be a bijection; the
sequence \(\sigma(1),\ldots,\sigma(16)\) specifies trail order. The opening and joining totals are
\[
A=\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}\mathbf a_i^T x_i,
\qquad
J=\sum_{k=1}^{15}
x_{\sigma(k)}^T C_{\sigma(k),\sigma(k+1)}
x_{\sigma(k+1)}.
\]
In this assembly rule, adjacent pieces are joined using the maximum
overlap of their exposed \(h\)-letter boundaries (at most \(h\) letters).
Each piece has length \(R_i+h+\mathbf a_i^Tx_i\); the join of consecutive
pieces removes \(h-D[E,S]\) repeated boundary letters. Therefore
\[
\begin{aligned}
L&=\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}\bigl(R_i+h+\mathbf a_i^T x_i\bigr)
   -\sum_{k=1}^{15}\bigl(h-
     x_{\sigma(k)}^T C_{\sigma(k),\sigma(k+1)}
     x_{\sigma(k+1)}\bigr)\\
 &=\boxed{h+\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}R_i+A+J}.
\end{aligned}
\]
This is an exact identity for the \emph{specified opening-and-joining
procedure}, not a claim of globally optimal overlap for arbitrary words.
Optimizing incoming and outgoing endpoints independently would generally
choose two different openings of the same trail and thus describe an
unrealizable assembly.

\section*{3. An expanded matrix from the new word}
The three weight-2 openings appear consecutively in trail order
\(14,15,13\). Trail IDs are zero-based identifiers in the saved plan;
offsets are positions in the corresponding cyclic spellings.
\[
\begin{array}{c|r|c|c|c}
\text{trail}&\text{offset}&g&S&E\\ \hline
14&6538&2&21750&17503\\
15&8616&2&17503&75032\\
13&3415&2&03254&32541
\end{array}
\]
Using the row order \(E_{14},E_{15},E_{13}\) and column order
\(S_{14},S_{15},S_{13}\), direct string comparison yields
\[
O=\begin{pmatrix}0&5&2\\1&0&3\\0&1&0\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
M=5\mathbf1\mathbf1^T-O
 =\begin{pmatrix}5&\boxed{0}&3\\4&5&\boxed{2}\\5&4&5\end{pmatrix},
\]
where \(\mathbf1=(1,1,1)^T\) and \(M\) is the restriction of \(D\) to
these specific boundary words. Diagonal entries are displayed for
completeness; the assembly never revisits a trail. Notice the direction:
\(M_{1,2}=0\), but \(M_{2,1}=4\).

The consecutive joins actually selected are
\[
\mathbf u_1^T M\mathbf u_2=0,
\qquad \mathbf u_2^T M\mathbf u_3=2,
\]
where \(\mathbf u_1=(1,0,0)^T\), \(\mathbf u_2=(0,1,0)^T\),
and \(\mathbf u_3=(0,0,1)^T\) index the displayed rows and columns.
The first join has the complete five-letter equality
\(E_{14}=S_{15}=17503\); the second uses the three-letter equality
\(\operatorname{suffix}_3(75032)=032
 =\operatorname{prefix}_3(03254)\), so it adds two boundary letters.
These are genuine entries for the reported openings; the total gain must
be counted across the complete sixteen-trail order.

\section*{4. Three extra opening letters save four joining letters}
The fixed total cyclic spelling length reported by the assembly plan is
\[
\sum_{i\in\mathcal T}R_i=46\,144
=(8!+7!+6!)+64.
\]
The extra 64 is part of the fixed trail data, not something derived by
this boundary-cost argument. Hence \(h+\sum_iR_i=46\,149\),
and the length accounting is
\[
\begin{array}{l|r|r}
 &\text{Halfar reference}&\text{new assembly}\\ \hline
h+\sum_iR_i&46\,149&46\,149\\
A&0&3\\
J&25&21\\ \hline
L&46\,174&46\,173
\end{array}
\]
For the new plan, the fifteen directed boundary costs are, in order,
\[
J=1+1+3+1+1+2+2+0+2+1+1+1+3+1+1=21.
\]
Consequently,
\[
\boxed{\Delta L=\Delta A+\Delta J=3+(21-25)=-1.}
\]
Three more letters are needed to open the selected trails, but four
fewer letters are needed to join the sixteen pieces. The net saving is
collective; it cannot be assigned independently to each changed opening.

\section*{5. Coverage, occupation, and scope}
For a linear word \(W\), let \(q_W(\pi)\) count its contiguous occurrences
of permutation \(\pi\), and define the \emph{occupied support}
\[
\operatorname{supp}(W)=\{\pi\in S_n:q_W(\pi)>0\}.
\]
Here \(S_n\) denotes the symmetric group, viewed as the set of all
permutations of the alphabet. In this note, \emph{occupation-preserving}
means that every permutation remains in this support; it does
\emph{not} mean that all multiplicities \(q_W(\pi)\) are unchanged.
All designated permutation windows are preserved inside their complete
opened pieces, and joining on equal substrings preserves those pieces.
Additional permutation windows spanning a join may change.

Independent sliding-window scans of the supplied reference and improved
words give \(8!=40\,320\) distinct permutations in each. The new word
has 56 repeated permutation occurrences, compared with 57 in the
reference, and both have 5,790 dirty windows. In particular,
\(46\,173-8+1=40\,320+56+5\,790\), as required.

This is an occupation-support-preserving change of boundary phase. The
matrix formulation explains the coupled costs; Halfar's randomized
\texttt{trailsearch} supplies the discrete choices reported in the plan.
The argument does not establish minimality or a general analytic selection
rule. A discrete Fourier transform diagonalizes the cyclic-shift operator
of an internal clock, but that fact alone does not diagonalize the directed
boundary matrix \(D\).

\smallskip
\noindent\textit{Symmetry remark.}
For every simultaneous relabeling of the alphabet
\(\pi\in S_n\), the boundary matrix obeys
\[
D[\pi(E),\pi(S)]=D[E,S].
\]
Thus \(D\) commutes with the natural \(S_n\)-action on boundary words.
This may allow a representation-theoretic block decomposition, but does
not on its own give a scalar Fourier diagonalization or solve the opening
selection problem.
