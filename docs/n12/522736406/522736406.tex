\vspace{-1.5em}
We improved the $522{,}736{,}409$ benchmark based on Halfar's E selection
by three symbols. The resulting $522{,}736{,}406$ word is
\href{https://superpermutations.com/w/W-0136}{W-0136}.
The contribution is a modified selection and connection-aware arrangement
inside Pantone's completion construction, not a new general construction.
The RLLB correction explains how candidates were assessed; the literal
word, rather than a lower-bound score, establishes the record.

\section*{Definitions and the two layers}
Follow Pantone's slices~\cite{pantone} and the preferred glossary
terms~\cite{glossary}. Here $K=n-2=10$, so a complete native selection has
$9!=362{,}880$ 2-loops. A row has starting ordering $x$ and visible length
$v$; $v=0$ in a native file means a loop without a row. Charge is
$Q=\sum_{v>0}(K-v)$. Let $T$ count completed closed trails.
The selection fixes these objects; the arrangement fixes openings, order,
and literal overlaps. For a realized word write
\[
L=F_3+Q+J,\qquad F_3=12!+11!+10!=522{,}547{,}200,
\]
where $J$ is net opening and joining cost. It is not generically $T$.

\section*{1. Halfar's correction and the revised RLLB}
The initial packet lift could reduce $Q+T$ while making actual joins much
more expensive. Halfar quantified the issue: one
comparison changed local $Q$ from 266 to 184 and $T$ from 55 to 125,
while measured joining cost changed from 140 to 379. His fitted trail and
empty-loop prices, and rank correlations 0.90 versus 0.61 for priced
versus plain-floor scores, concern tested families, not universal constants.
The positions and chains of loops without rows matter as well as their count.

The updated global connector trie prices compatible endpoint words, with
one legal opening phase supplying \emph{both} ends of each trail.
For the restricted model of one intact weight-3 opening per trail,
put $h=n-3=9$ and let $O$ be total overlap. Then
\[
L=F_3+Q+hT-O.
\]
Give each exact connector key $a$ at depth $m\leq k<h$ a potential
$\theta_{k,a}$. For a legal phase $p$ of trail $i$ define
\[
B_i(p,\theta)=
\max\!\left\{\frac{m-1}{2},\max_k\left(\frac{k}{2}-\theta_{k,\mathrm{pre}_k(p)}\right)\right\}
+\max\!\left\{\frac{m-1}{2},\max_k\left(\frac{k}{2}+\theta_{k,\mathrm{suf}_k(p)}\right)\right\},
\]
where an unavailable incoming or outgoing match is omitted. Set
$U(\theta)=\sum_i\max_p B_i(p,\theta)$.
For an actual matched key, its two potential contributions cancel.
Fallback values overestimate shorter overlaps, and a zero-overlap dummy
closing edge handles path endpoints. Thus $O\leq U(\theta)$ and
\[
L\geq F_3+Q+9T-\lceil U(\theta)\rceil
\]
with conservative numerical rounding in the implementation.

For the Q210 control, the saved prices give $Q=179{,}424$, $T=6{,}624$,
$U\leq49{,}770$, $J\geq9{,}846$, and $L\geq522{,}736{,}470$.
That rejects this control against its historical $522{,}736{,}428$ target
\emph{only inside the intact weight-3 model}. Weight-2 openings, splits,
and unrestricted words are not rejected by that certificate.

\section*{2. Change closed-walk footprints, not just isolated blocks}
E has 48 independently closed residual fibers and a connected core spanning
672 other fibers. Independent residual blocks can be relabeled while
preserving their assigned support. Arbitrary one-fiber core replacements
need not preserve closure. We therefore considered complete closed-walk
footprints and compound replacements.

The successful initial core selection has $Q=182{,}632$, $T=2{,}904$.
From 1,306 balanced exchange alternatives, two compound replacements,
each involving five original walks, change 28 row assignments in total.
They add 80 charge units and remove 72 completed trails. Joint phase and
placement evaluation takes a $522{,}736{,}550$ arrangement to
$522{,}736{,}530$. The final selection has $Q=182{,}712$ and $T=2{,}832$:
2,352 trails from loops without rows and 480 active completion trails.

\section*{3. Opening and arrangement search}
For a fixed order and finite opening states $s$, use
\[
D_i(s)=|W_i(s)|+\min_t\left[D_{i-1}(t)-\operatorname{ov}(W_{i-1}(t),W_i(s))\right].
\]
The state library includes openings at weights 3, 2, and 1 and can use
literal overlaps through 15 symbols. These freedoms exceed the restricted
RLLB state space. The outer search changes order and reopens affected
phase choices. Segment assignments are patched to eliminate subtours;
segment insertion, larger successor exchanges, iterated local search,
and neutral moves provide further arrangements.

The final arrangement passes $522{,}736{,}495$, 479, 472, 467, 443, and
423, then ties 409 before the last reconnection reaches 406.
The saved plan has 2,832 entries. Opening overhangs total 25,557 and
overlaps total 19,063, giving $J=6,494$.

\section*{5. Current reproduction and attribution}
The first chain compiles and runs the new \path{search/rllb.cpp}, via
\path{build/rllb}, on the actual final 406 selection. It is separate from
the Q210 control above. The second chain exactly decodes the pinned SPRA
witness and checks its canonical Almanac hash. The original 2,832-row
plan is retained, but its \texttt{base}, \texttt{cand0}, and \texttt{cand3}
source catalogues are absent. A self-contained historical selection-to-plan
materialization or discovery search is therefore not claimed.

Pantone supplied the construction and Halfar the E selection and generalized
native machinery. This work supplies the compound selection changes,
phase-coupled scoring, and arrangement experiment, with AI assistance.
The Almanac separately credits later improvements to Pantone and Halfar:
W-0137 (195), W-0140 (146), W-0142 (145), W-0144 (144), W-0146 (133), and
W-0150 (132), where each suffix follows $522{,}736{,}$~\cite{almanac}.
Those later results build on this line but are not claimed here.
